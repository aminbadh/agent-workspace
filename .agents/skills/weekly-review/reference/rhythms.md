# Weekly Rhythms

The menu onboarding offers when it sets up the user's weekly review. Each method gets a few lines: what it is, who it fits, its planning and reflection steps, and where its plan lives, which the one-thing skill reads. Suggest one or two from the user's habits, never the whole list. If the user names a method that is not here, search for it first (AGENTS.md, rule 7) and write its steps in the same shape.

**Questions that pick a method:** do they plan now, and how (paper, an app, in their head)? Which day suits a review, and how long can it take? Do they like numbers and scores, or do numbers feel like pressure? Is their life mostly one big goal, several areas at once, or a stream of incoming tasks?

**Wherever the method keeps its plan, the week's plan goes to the task home** (see AGENTS.md, Tasks), so there is one place to look. In a TASKS.md, that is its "This week" section.

## GTD weekly review

David Allen's *Getting Things Done*. Everything that has your attention gets captured, decided and filed as a next action, a project, something waited on, or someday.

- **Fits:** people with many small incoming commitments (email, requests, errands) who feel things slipping through, and who like lists more than goals.
- **Plan:** get clear (empty every inbox and loose note into the task home, each item decided), get current (read the next actions, the projects list, waiting-for, and the calendar two weeks back and two ahead), get creative (read someday, promote what is now live).
- **Reflect:** which projects have no next action? Add one, or admit it is someday.
- **Plan lives in:** the next actions in the task home, grouped by where they can be done.
- **Time:** an hour or more the first weeks, then about forty minutes.

## Agile Results

J.D. Meier's *Getting Results the Agile Way*, built on the Rule of 3: three outcomes for the day, the week, the month and the year.

- **Fits:** people who want something light, with no system to maintain, and who think in wins rather than tasks.
- **Plan (Monday Vision):** pick three wins for the week, as outcomes ("the application is sent"), not activities. Each morning, three outcomes for the day.
- **Reflect (Friday Reflection):** three things that went well, three to improve. Carry the lesson into next Monday's wins.
- **Plan lives in:** the week's three wins, in the task home's This week.
- **Time:** ten minutes on Monday, ten on Friday.

## 12 Week Year

Brian Moran and Michael Lennington's *The 12 Week Year*. The year is too long to feel urgent, so plan in twelve-week blocks with a weekly score.

- **Fits:** people with one or two big goals and a deadline feel, who like numbers and want accountability.
- **Plan:** a vision, then two or three goals for the twelve weeks, each broken into tactics (weekly actions). Each week, list that week's tactics.
- **Reflect:** score the past week: tactics done out of tactics planned. 85% is the target; below it, ask what got in the way. Week 13 reviews the block and plans the next.
- **Plan lives in:** the week's planned tactics, in the task home's This week; the twelve-week goals and tactics as a project in the task home.
- **Time:** twenty minutes a week, a longer session every twelfth week.

## Multi-scale planning

Cal Newport's method: a plan at each scale, each read when making the next one down.

- **Fits:** students and people with deep work to protect, who are willing to plan their days in time blocks.
- **Plan:** a plan for the quarter or semester (the few big things), reviewed weekly to write the week's plan (what gets done, the calendar's fixed points), and each morning a time-blocked plan for the day.
- **Reflect:** did the week's plan survive contact? Adjust the quarter if a big thing keeps slipping.
- **Plan lives in:** today's time blocks (in the calendar or a notebook, so ask), then the week's plan in the task home's This week; the quarter plan as a project in the task home.
- **Time:** thirty minutes a week, five minutes a day.

## Bullet Journal migration

Ryder Carroll's *The Bullet Journal Method*, usually on paper: rapid logging, daily logs, a monthly log, and migration, where every open task is rewritten or dropped.

- **Fits:** people who think best on paper, and anyone who wants the act of rewriting to kill tasks that do not matter.
- **Plan:** go through the week's daily logs. For each open task: done, migrated (rewritten into the coming week or this month's list), scheduled into the future log, or struck as irrelevant. At the month's start, set up the monthly log the same way.
- **Reflect:** what keeps getting migrated? Do it, schedule it, or drop it.
- **Plan lives in:** the migrated tasks for the coming week, copied into the task home's This week so the agent can see them, since the journal itself is on paper. If the user keeps the journal digitally, link it from PROFILE.md.
- **Time:** fifteen to thirty minutes a week, longer at the month's turn.

## The Compass

Gary Keller's *The ONE Thing*, applied across the areas of a life: each area has a goal ladder from Someday down to this month, and the week serves the Monthly rungs.

- **Fits:** people juggling several areas (studies, work, health, family) who worry about one starving the others, and who want help choosing between them.
- **Set up:** install [COMPASS.md](../assets/COMPASS.md) at the workspace root. Show the life areas and let the user pick, rename or merge the ones that matter (six to eight is usual, five is plenty): career or work, business, finances, health, family and friends, romance, personal growth, fun and recreation, physical environment, contribution, spirituality. Fill each ladder with the user, marking anything suggested *(proposed)*. Scores are optional and off for a first setup; turn them on later by adding a Score column (out of 5, measuring health, not priority).
- **Plan:** every week, read the Monthly rungs and pick one to three actions that move them, favouring areas that never generate deadlines (health, relationships). Check the task home for a fitting task before creating one, and confirm the list with the user.
- **Reflect, when COMPASS.md is due (monthly):** for each Monthly rung, did it happen? Raise it if achieved, keep it if it never got a chance, replace it if overtaken, deriving the new one from the 1-Year rung. A live workstream with no rung pointing at it is drifting: name it. A rung explaining what it does not do has become a decision log; move the reasoning to the domain. Rescore if scores are on. Bump `last_reviewed`.
- **Plan lives in:** the Monthly rungs in COMPASS.md, and the week's actions in the task home's This week.
- **Time:** fifteen minutes a week, forty-five once a month.
