# AI Writing Tells

Patterns that make text read as machine-written. Onboarding offers this list; the user picks which to avoid, and AGENTS.md's "Working with me" names their choice and links here. Each tell costs most in copy that has to read as a real person: posts, emails, bios, applications, page text.

## In sentences

- **Em dashes.** The most recognised tell. Use a colon, a period, a comma or parentheses; if none fits, restructure the sentence.
- **"It's not just X, it's Y"** and its variants ("not only... but", "this isn't about X, it's about Y").
- **Three abstract nouns in a row** ("clarity, structure and impact"). A list of three concrete things is fine.
- **Rhetorical questions as openers** ("Ever wondered why...?").
- **Inflated verbs and adjectives:** delve, leverage, unlock, elevate, seamless, robust, game-changer, tapestry, landscape, journey.
- **Hedge stacks:** "it's worth noting that", "it's important to remember", "arguably".

## In structure

- **A closing paragraph that restates what was just said** ("In summary...", "Ultimately...").
- **Every paragraph the same length and shape,** or every point a bullet with a bold lead.
- **An opener that praises the question or restates the request** before answering.
- **Hard line breaks inside paragraphs** in anything to be pasted elsewhere: they survive into email, forms and apps as ragged half-lines. Keep each paragraph on one line and break only between paragraphs, list items, addresses and sign-offs.

## How to use it

Avoid the chosen tells in anything written for others. In replies to the user, follow their "Working with me" preferences, which may be looser. When a tell seems like the only option, rewrite the sentence rather than keep it.
